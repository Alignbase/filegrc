# Anti-Bribery and Corruption Policy

## Purpose

{{company_name}} conducts business honestly and complies with applicable anti-bribery and anti-corruption laws. No business result justifies offering, requesting, accepting, or concealing an improper payment or benefit.

## Scope

This policy applies to employees, contractors, officers, directors, agents, consultants, and third parties acting for {{company_name}}.

## Prohibited conduct

Covered persons must not directly or indirectly:

- Offer, promise, authorize, give, request, or accept anything of value to improperly influence a decision.
- Reward someone for misusing a position of trust.
- Use a third party to do something this policy prohibits.
- Make facilitation or expediting payments, except when necessary to prevent an immediate threat to health or safety.
- Create false, incomplete, misleading, or undisclosed records.
- Retaliate against someone who raises a concern in good faith.

"Anything of value" includes money, gifts, meals, travel, entertainment, discounts, services, employment opportunities, charitable contributions, political contributions, and benefits provided to a recipient's family or associates.

## Government officials

Interactions with government officials require added care. The term includes elected and appointed officials, employees of government agencies or state-controlled entities, political candidates, public international organizations, and anyone acting in an official capacity.

No payment, gift, or benefit may be offered to a government official to obtain or retain business, avoid a requirement, influence an official act, or secure an improper advantage.

## Gifts, meals, travel, and entertainment

Business courtesies must:

- Have a legitimate business purpose.
- Be reasonable, infrequent, and appropriate to the circumstances.
- Comply with the recipient's rules and applicable law.
- Never consist of cash or a cash equivalent.
- Never be intended to influence a pending decision.
- Be recorded accurately when {{company_name}} pays for them.

The policy owner must approve any courtesy that could reasonably appear improper.

## Political and charitable contributions

Company funds, property, or services may not be used for political contributions without written approval from the policy owner and confirmation that the contribution is lawful.

Charitable contributions must have a documented charitable purpose and may not benefit a decision-maker personally or act as a substitute for an improper payment.

## Third parties

Before engaging an agent, consultant, reseller, or other intermediary who may interact with customers or officials on {{company_name}}'s behalf, the responsible owner must:

1. Perform risk-based due diligence.
2. Confirm the service and compensation are commercially reasonable.
3. Use a written agreement describing the service and requiring lawful conduct.
4. Approve and retain invoices and supporting records.
5. Monitor for unusual requests, payment methods, or relationships.

Warning signs must be resolved before engagement or payment. Examples include:

- A request for payment to an unrelated person, unusual account, or country unrelated to the work.
- Vague services, excessive commissions, or invoices that do not match the agreement.
- A close relationship with a government official or decision-maker.
- Refusal to complete due diligence or sign appropriate contract terms.
- A request to hide a person's identity, role, or the purpose of a transaction.

## Books and records

Transactions must be recorded promptly, accurately, and with enough detail to explain their purpose. Undisclosed accounts, false descriptions, fabricated invoices, and off-book transactions are prohibited.

## Reporting

Report questions, suspected violations, requests for improper payments, or inaccurate records to {{policy_owner_name}} at {{security_contact_email}}. Reports may be made without first notifying a manager.

{{company_name}} prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports a concern or participates in an investigation in good faith.

## Investigations and enforcement

{{company_name}} will review credible reports promptly and limit disclosure to people who need the information. Covered persons must preserve relevant records and cooperate honestly.

Violations may result in removal of authority, termination of a contract or employment relationship, recovery of funds, and referral to authorities when appropriate.

## Training and review

People in higher-risk roles complete assigned anti-bribery training within 30 days of starting those duties or changing into a covered role. Covered roles include work involving sales, procurement, payments, gifts or hospitality, government interaction, higher-risk locations, or third parties acting for {{company_name}}. The onboarding or role-change checklist records the training assignment or why it does not apply.

This policy is reviewed at least annually and after a material legal, geographic, or business change.
