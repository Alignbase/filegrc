# Security Awareness and Incident Response Training

## Purpose

Security depends on the choices people make while using accounts, devices, data, and company systems. This training explains the common warning signs, the safeguards everyone must use, and how to report a suspected incident.

This training applies to employees and contractors who use {{company_name}} systems or information. Complete it within 30 days after starting, at least annually, and again when assigned after a material change or incident.

Questions and incident reports use the current approved security reporting channel delivered with this assignment.

## Your responsibilities

Everyone with access to company information must:

- Follow the policies and procedures assigned to their work.
- Use only approved accounts, devices, applications, storage, and communications services.
- Protect credentials, authentication factors, devices, paper records, and conversations.
- Access only the systems and data needed for assigned work.
- Stop and verify an unexpected or sensitive request.
- Report suspected loss, misuse, compromise, or policy violations immediately.

You do not need proof that an incident occurred before reporting it. A prompt report gives the response team more time to limit harm.

## Common threats

### Phishing and social engineering

Attackers may use email, text messages, calls, meeting invitations, shared documents, login pages, or personal contact to obtain information or cause an unsafe action.

Be cautious when a message:

- Creates unusual urgency, fear, secrecy, or pressure.
- Requests a password, authentication code, payment, gift card, sensitive file, or access change.
- Comes from an unexpected address, domain, phone number, or account.
- Contains an unexpected attachment, link, QR code, or shared document.
- Changes established payment or account instructions.
- Claims to be a manager, customer, vendor, support person, or government official but cannot be verified through a known channel.

Do not rely on a familiar display name, logo, writing style, caller ID, website padlock, or message history as proof of identity. Verify unusual requests through a separate trusted channel. Report suspected phishing through the approved process.

### Account compromise

Warning signs include:

- An unexpected multi-factor authentication prompt or password-reset message.
- A login alert from an unfamiliar place or device.
- Messages, rules, files, transactions, or access changes you did not create.
- A password manager or identity-system warning.
- A coworker or customer reporting unusual activity from your account.

Deny unexpected authentication prompts and report them. Never approve a prompt to make repeated notifications stop.

### Malware and unsafe software

Warning signs include unexpected security alerts, disabled safeguards, new applications or browser extensions, unusual pop-ups, unexplained system changes, unexpected restarts, missing files, or abnormal network or device behavior.

Do not install unapproved software or follow instructions from an unexpected pop-up. Do not attempt to remove suspected malware yourself unless the response team instructs you to do so.

### Data misuse and insider risk

Security concerns may involve a person inside or outside the company. Focus on observable actions, such as:

- Trying to access information unrelated to assigned work.
- Copying, collecting, or transferring an unusual amount of data.
- Sharing credentials or bypassing an approval or security control.
- Sending company data to an unapproved account, device, or service.
- Concealing a transaction, access change, or system action.
- Threatening people, systems, facilities, or records.

Report the action or event. Do not investigate a coworker, search their records, or make assumptions about their motives.

### Physical loss or unauthorized access

Report a lost or stolen device, badge, authentication token, paper record, or removable medium. Also report unauthorized entry, broken access controls, missing records, or a visitor in a restricted area without an escort.

## Identity and authentication

- Use a unique identity for company work. Do not share accounts.
- Use a unique password for each account and store it only through the credential-protection method approved for that System.
- Prefer long, randomly generated passwords or long passphrases. Do not make predictable substitutions or reuse passwords.
- Keep plaintext passwords, recovery codes, private keys, and authentication tokens out of email, chat, tickets, source code, and general-purpose documents. Approved source-controlled ciphertext must follow the Policy and keep decryption keys separate.
- Use multi-factor authentication when required.
- Protect authentication devices and report an unexpected prompt, lost factor, or suspected credential exposure.
- Never disclose a password or authentication code to someone who asks for it.

If a system forces a specific password format, follow that system's rules. Length and uniqueness matter more than adding a predictable capital letter, number, or symbol.

## Devices and software

Use a company-managed device for company work unless another arrangement is approved.

- Keep the operating system, browser, applications, and security tools supported and updated.
- Enable automatic security updates when approved.
- Keep disk encryption, screen locking, malware protection, firewall, logging, and device-management controls enabled.
- Lock the screen whenever the device is unattended. Company-managed devices must use the approved automatic-lock setting.
- Use a standard user account for routine work. Use administrative access only when authorized and needed.
- Install applications and browser extensions only through an approved process.
- Do not connect unapproved removable media.
- Keep devices under your control in public places and while traveling.
- Report loss, theft, compromise, or unexpected reconfiguration immediately.

Do not wipe, reset, power off, or materially alter a suspected compromised device unless the response team instructs you to do so.

## Networks, remote work, and mobile devices

- Use trusted networks or an approved encrypted connection.
- Treat public and shared networks as untrusted.
- Use approved remote-access methods and multi-factor authentication when the Policy, Control, customer commitment, or risk decision requires it.
- Do not discuss or display confidential information where another person can see or hear it.
- Keep work data out of personal accounts and unapproved applications.
- Use approved mobile applications, device encryption, screen locking, and remote lock or wipe where supported.
- Remove local copies of company data when the approved work no longer requires them.

## Email, messaging, web use, and social media

- Use approved company communications accounts for company work.
- Verify recipients before sending sensitive information.
- Confirm the classification and approved transfer method before sharing Confidential or Restricted data.
- Preview or inspect destinations before opening links. When in doubt, navigate through a known bookmark or type the known address yourself.
- Treat unexpected attachments and shared documents as suspicious until verified.
- Do not enter credentials after following an unexpected link.
- Do not approve browser warnings or install software to satisfy an unexpected website or pop-up.
- Do not publish company, customer, workforce, vendor, security, or location information without authorization.

A secure connection protects data in transit. It does not prove that a site, sender, or request is legitimate.

## Data handling

Follow the Information Security Policy and use the highest applicable classification.

- Collect, access, and retain only the data needed for approved work.
- Store company data only in approved systems.
- Use least privilege and approved sharing settings.
- Do not use production data in development or testing unless approved and equally protected.
- Do not put plaintext credentials, authentication tokens, or cryptographic keys in source repositories or general-purpose Systems. Approved source-controlled ciphertext must meet the Policy's separate-key, access, and rotation conditions.
- Dispose of paper, devices, and media through an approved process.
- Report unintended disclosure, excessive access, improper disposal, or an unexpected public link.

## Building and changing systems

People who design, build, review, deploy, or administer company Systems must:

- Keep code, infrastructure changes, and production access in approved repositories and workflows.
- Protect branches and deployments with the reviews, tests, and approvals required by the Change Management Control.
- Keep plaintext secrets out of source code, build output, tickets, chat, and logs. Repository access alone must never decrypt approved source-controlled ciphertext.
- Validate input, authorization, error handling, and sensitive-data use at trust boundaries.
- Review new and changed dependencies, resolve security findings within the approved risk targets, and record Exceptions when a target cannot be met.
- Separate development and production duties when practical. Record a compensating or post-deployment review when team size or urgency prevents independent pre-deployment review.
- Record emergency changes, limit their scope, validate the result, and complete the required follow-up review.

Approved supporting standards, procedures, and schedules document the tools, settings, approval paths, and evidence.

## Physical security and clear workspaces

- Use your own badge, key, and authentication factor. Do not allow another person to follow you into a restricted area without authorization.
- Confirm that visitors follow the applicable check-in and escort process.
- Lock screens and secure sensitive papers whenever a workspace is unattended.
- Retrieve sensitive print jobs promptly and use approved secure disposal.
- Keep devices, badges, keys, and removable media under your control.
- Protect screens and conversations from casual viewing or overhearing.

## Recognizing a possible incident

Report any event that could affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of company systems or information. Examples include:

- Suspected phishing, fraud, malware, or credential exposure
- Unauthorized access or an unexpected access change
- Lost or stolen equipment, badges, records, or authentication factors
- Data sent to the wrong person or made public
- Missing, altered, or unexplained files, logs, or transactions
- Unexpected system behavior, security alerts, or service outages
- A customer, vendor, or worker reporting possible misuse or incorrect data
- A broken physical or technical security control
- A serious policy violation that could create security or privacy harm

## Reporting and first actions

1. Stop the unsafe action. Do not continue entering credentials, sending data, or following the request.
2. Report the event immediately through the approved security reporting channel delivered with this assignment. If that channel is unavailable, use the current approved fallback channel.
3. State what happened, when it happened, the affected account, device, system, or data, and any action already taken.
4. Preserve the message, file, screen, device, or other evidence. Take a screenshot only when it is safe and does not expose more sensitive data.
5. Follow response-team instructions. Remain available for questions.

Do not:

- Delay reporting while trying to prove or fix the issue.
- Delete messages, files, logs, or other evidence.
- Forward suspicious content broadly.
- Contact an attacker, suspected person, customer, regulator, press outlet, or law-enforcement agency unless authorized.
- Promise an outcome or describe the event outside the response process.

## What happens after a report

The response team determines the event's scope and severity. It coordinates containment, evidence preservation, recovery, required communications, and follow-up work. Material incidents receive a root-cause and lessons review within one week.

The person who reports an event should cooperate with the response but should not classify, investigate, contain, or communicate the incident unless assigned that role.

The acknowledgement records the exact training revision reviewed and any signed evidence required for the assignment.
